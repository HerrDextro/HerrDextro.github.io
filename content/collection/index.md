## Oil lamps

I admire old simple devices. Oil lamps, especially of the cold blast variant, are very ingenious designs that are ultra reliable and bulletproof. I also just like the glow of a real flame.

I like having things that work when infrastructure goes down. A lot of what I do is oriented in this direction (decentralized mesh networks, radio, local AI).

![Three of my hurricane lanterns side by side.](lanterns-size-comparison.jpg)

- Small 1930s reflector lamp
- Small standing oil lamp with glass tank
- Lampe Pigeon wall-mounted wick oil lamp
- KOSMOS burner large standing oil lamp made of copper and brass, polished from terrible state to perfection
- Feuerhand Super Baby No. 175
- Dietz No. 80 Blizzard clone with black Hammerite finish
- SIF 500 in good state
- Antique roadworks site oil lamp

## Night vision

Another one of my obsessions is night vision. When I was maybe 13, I was playing a game of tag with friends during the night. Then one time I must've messed up my flashlight discipline, and I promised my friends I would get night vision one day.

At 16 I moved to realize that vision, BUT one issue: night vision is extremely expensive. So I got the best thing I could afford: surplus Soviet night vision. It may kinda suck, but I can still see at night.

### NS-71, Polish sapper night vision

![My NS-71 set in its open wooden case](nv-ns71-case-contents.jpg)

This is a generation 0 active IR night vision set. It consists of a set of binoculars, a large IR spotlight made from a car lamp (this is how it comes the standard way) mounted on top of the binoculars, and then a transformer plus battery case carried at the waist that connects to the binoculars and spotlight by a long cable.

This was the first set I got and only the left eye worked, but nevertheless it is actually very usable. It comes in a nice wooden case together with many spares, a carry bag and battery compartments.

![Mounted on a Swiss Army helmet. Originally it was intended for a Warsaw Pact M73 helmet.](nv-ns71-helmet.jpg)

![The NS-71's wooden case, closed](nv-ns71-case.jpg)

### PNV-57 driver's goggles with helmet

![My PNV-57 on its tanker helmet, next to the steel case](nv-pnv57-helmet.jpg)

This is a generation 0 active IR NV binocular permanently attached to a Soviet-style tanker helmet, with a transformer on the back of the helmet and a power cord running down from it.

The performance under natural light or an 850 nm flashlight is utterly terrible, but I can imagine that with a few kilowatts worth of high-pass filtered Soviet truck light it would perform well enough to drive. It comes in a nice steel case containing some maintenance parts and some circular high-pass IR filter glasses to put over a truck's headlights to convert them to IR.

### PNV-57E

![My PNV-57E](nv-pnv57e.jpg)

This is a passive gen 1 ambient light amplifying driver's goggle set. Normally it would come in the same configuration as the PNV-57, with a driver's helmet plus transformer on the back. In this case, I bought it as a lone unit without even any transformer, and built/tested several high voltage power supplies for it (these Soviet units need anywhere from 17.5 to 19.5 kV (!) depending on the model).

This one works very well, but one of the wires broke, so now only one eye works (I am working on finding a replacement). The standard focus is from 3 meters to infinity, which is optimized for drivers. The large apertures on this device also cause the depth of focus to be narrower than for example with the NS-71, so that is also a disadvantage (but they do let more light in!).

This unit draws only a fraction of the power of the active ones, since they all need some form of IR spotlight and this one doesn't necessarily (I recommend using it with IR though).

### Fero D51 (tubeless)

![My Fero D51 from the front](nv-fero-d51-front.jpg)

This is a Fero D51, popularized by special forces and movies like Aliens, AKA the Colonial Marine Space Binos. I bought it to build my very own homebrew NV binocular based on the Soviet 1PN63, but didn't have the heart to completely disassemble it yet. So I am waiting to buy another suitable donor NVG for the lenses required to make my DIY night vision.

![The Fero D51 from the side](nv-fero-d51-side.jpg)

### NVG face mask

![The 3D printed face mask with the mounting block](nv-facemask-front.jpg)

I modelled and 3D printed this face mask while on a hiking camp in Graubünden, so I could use my NVG more comfortably. It consists of a main 3D printed part and uses improvised straps made from Spanngurt (tie-down straps). I also bought a VR headset padding kit to put on it to make it more comfortable. It has a temporary mounting block on it to use with the NS-71.

![The back of the mask, with the VR headset padding and the straps](nv-facemask-back.jpg)

## Military gear

I also collect other military gear for lighting, power, rigging, clothing etc.

### Generators

![The small 200 W generator](genset-200w-front.jpg)

I have two generators. The large 400 W one is intended for the Swiss Army B200 spotlight set, the small 200 W one for an area lighting set (Platzbeleuchtungsset). Both are well preserved, but needed some work (carb cleaning).

![The 200 W generator from the other side](genset-200w-rear.jpg)

![The 200 W generator in its cover](genset-200w-covered.jpg)

### Swiss Army pickaxe and shovel

![Swiss Army sapper shovel and mountaineering pickaxe from 1943](mil-pickaxe-shovel.jpg)

This is a sapper shovel and mountaineering pickaxe made in 1943 for use in the Swiss Army. The condition is still very good and I use them for digging small foxholes.

### Tilley/Birchmeier spotlight

![The Tilley/Birchmeier spotlight on its stand](lamp-tilley-birchmeier.jpg)

This is a very large (approximately 180–190 cm high) gas evaporation lamp used by the Swiss Zivilschutz. I bought it because 1: I love lamps (obviously) and 2: I am constructing a bunker in Belgium with some friends, where this really comes in handy.

![The lamp head and the gas tank up close](lamp-tilley-birchmeier-closeup.jpg)
